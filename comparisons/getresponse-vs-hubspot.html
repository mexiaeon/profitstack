GetResponse vs. HubSpot: Email Marketing or CRM?

Software comparison · Email campaigns, customer records, automation, free tools and pricing

GetResponse and HubSpot both help businesses communicate with leads and customers, but they start from different jobs. GetResponse is the more natural starting point for email campaigns and marketing automation. HubSpot is the stronger starting point when you need a shared CRM to organize contacts, deals and sales activity.

That distinction matters more than a simple feature count. A newsletter business may not need a large CRM suite. A sales team may quickly outgrow an email platform that is not designed to manage a full deal pipeline. This guide compares the products around those real workflows so you can shortlist the right one before paying.

1. What each platform is built to do

GetResponse: build and communicate with an audience

GetResponse centers its product around email marketing. Typical workflows include creating signup forms, collecting subscribers, sending newsletters, setting up welcome messages and segmenting an audience. Depending on the plan, it also offers more advanced automation, landing pages, ecommerce-focused tools, sales funnels, webinars and creator features.

Its contact management is useful for organizing subscribers and campaign activity. However, a business that needs a full sales process—with deal stages, sales-owner tasks, pipeline reporting and a shared customer history—should check whether it needs a separate CRM rather than assuming GetResponse replaces one.

HubSpot: keep customer and sales activity in one CRM

HubSpot starts with its Smart CRM and adds separate product Hubs for marketing, sales, service, content and other functions. Its free CRM tools include contact, deal and task management, email tracking, meeting scheduling, live chat and reporting dashboards. This makes it useful even before a business buys advanced marketing features.

The trade-off is that HubSpot is a broad platform with multiple product tiers. A feature may belong to a specific Hub or require a higher subscription. Before buying, map the tools you actually need—especially marketing automation, reporting and team permissions—to the right Hub and tier.

2. Email marketing and automation

GetResponse has the more direct email-marketing proposition. Its paid plans list unlimited monthly email sends, and its plan structure makes automation limits relatively easy to identify: Starter includes one custom automation workflow, while Marketer includes unlimited workflows and advanced audience segmentation. Marketer also adds ecommerce-oriented features such as abandoned-cart recovery and sales funnels.

HubSpot connects email to CRM information. That can be valuable when marketers need to use sales and customer data to decide who receives a message. The free tools include up to 2,000 marketing email sends per calendar month with HubSpot branding. Starter adds more email capacity and limited simple automation. More advanced workflow automation is reserved for eligible Professional and Enterprise subscriptions.

GetResponse may suit you when

  • Email campaigns and subscriber journeys are the main work
  • You want landing pages, forms and email tools in the same product
  • You need more than one automated email workflow and can budget for Marketer
  • Starter has a one-workflow limit
  • Pricing rises with subscriber-list size

HubSpot may suit you when

  • Email activity needs to connect to sales and customer records
  • You want to begin with a free CRM and add marketing tools later
  • Your team values shared contact history and reporting
  • Free email sends are limited and include HubSpot branding
  • Advanced workflows require higher-tier subscriptions

3. Free plans and paid pricing

The free options are not equivalent. HubSpot's free CRM is designed to be an ongoing entry point for contact and deal management. GetResponse offers a free account with restrictions and a 14-day period of premium-feature access; after that trial, premium capabilities are no longer available unless you upgrade.

GetResponse pricing snapshot

For a list of up to 1,000 subscribers, GetResponse currently lists Starter at $19 per month when paid monthly, or about $15.58 per month when billed annually. Marketer is listed at $59 per month monthly, or about $48.38 per month on annual billing. Annual figures are monthly equivalents paid in advance, not month-to-month prices.

Starter includes one custom automation workflow. Marketer is the tier to examine if you need unlimited workflows, advanced segmentation or ecommerce automation. GetResponse pricing is affected by list size, so use the provider's calculator for your actual subscriber count before comparing it with another vendor.

HubSpot pricing snapshot

HubSpot's free tools are listed at $0 for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with no time limit. The free Marketing Hub tools include up to 2,000 marketing email sends per calendar month, with HubSpot branding. That can be enough for a small business testing a CRM and sending occasional campaigns.

HubSpot currently advertises promotional Starter pricing from $7 per seat per month for eligible new customers, while its standard monthly Starter price is shown at $20 per seat per month. Promotions and billing terms can change. For a sense of the jump to advanced marketing automation, Marketing Hub Professional is currently listed from $800 per month on annual billing, with 3 Core Seats and a required one-time onboarding fee listed at $3,000. Confirm the current quote and contact tier directly before committing.

4. Which platform should you choose?

Choose GetResponse if your business is email-first

Start with GetResponse if you primarily want to collect subscribers, publish newsletters, build welcome sequences and automate campaign follow-ups. It is also worth considering if you sell online and want to evaluate its higher-tier ecommerce features. For more than one meaningful automation journey, compare Marketer rather than assuming Starter will be enough.

Choose HubSpot if your business is sales- and CRM-first

Start with HubSpot if you need a structured place to track prospects, companies, deals, sales activity and tasks. Its free CRM tools may be a sensible first step for a solo operator or small team that wants to organize leads before paying for marketing software. Upgrade only when a specific paid feature is needed.

Choose neither until you calculate the real cost

If your requirements include a large contact database, several users, complex automation and multiple integrations, build a one-year cost estimate for both options. Include the number of marketing contacts, sending volume, seats, onboarding, optional add-ons and the plan required for each workflow. The cheapest starting price may not be the cheapest system for your actual use.

5. A practical checklist before you subscribe

  • Write down your workflow: Is the main job sending campaigns, or tracking leads through a sales pipeline?
  • Count contacts correctly: Estimate subscribers for GetResponse and marketing contacts for HubSpot, not just total records in a spreadsheet.
  • Map automation: List the trigger, conditions and actions for each workflow you need. Check which tier includes every step.
  • Count users and permissions: Include every person who needs access, not just the account owner.
  • Test a real process: Import a small sample, build one campaign or form, test a follow-up, and check the reporting.
  • Review the contract: Confirm billing frequency, renewal price, taxes, cancellation terms, data export and any mandatory onboarding.

Frequently asked questions

Is GetResponse a CRM like HubSpot?

Not in the same sense. GetResponse provides contact and subscriber management for marketing campaigns. HubSpot's free CRM is specifically designed to manage contacts, deals, tasks and customer activity. If sales pipeline management is essential, evaluate HubSpot or another dedicated CRM.

Can I use HubSpot for free?

Yes. HubSpot lists free tools for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts with no time limit. Its free marketing email allowance is up to 2,000 sends per calendar month and includes HubSpot branding. Check the free-plan feature table for other limits before building a process around it.

Is GetResponse cheaper than HubSpot?

It depends on what you are comparing. HubSpot's free CRM can cost nothing for a small team that needs basic contact and deal management. GetResponse's paid Starter plan is a clearer fit for businesses that need a dedicated email-marketing product and unlimited monthly sends. HubSpot costs can rise when you add paid seats, marketing contacts or advanced Marketing Hub features.

Which one has better automation?

For email-led automation, GetResponse is easier to evaluate: Starter has one custom workflow and Marketer includes unlimited workflows. HubSpot connects automation to CRM records, but full workflow automation requires eligible Professional or Enterprise subscriptions. The better fit depends on whether you are automating subscriber campaigns or broader sales and customer processes.

Which is better for a small agency?

A small agency focused on newsletters and campaign delivery may prefer GetResponse. An agency managing its own prospect pipeline and client relationships may prefer HubSpot's CRM. If you intend to manage separate client environments, check account structure, permissions, reporting and total seat costs before choosing.
