MailerLite vs. Brevo: which email platform fits your business?

Email marketing comparison · Free-plan limits, automation, contact storage, and costs

MailerLite and Brevo both offer free plans, but they structure their limits differently. MailerLite's free plan is built around a smaller active-subscriber allowance and a monthly email quota. Brevo lets you store a much larger contact database for free, but caps free email campaigns at 300 sends per day.

That difference matters more than a headline feature list. If you have a small newsletter and send a few campaigns each month, MailerLite may feel more straightforward. If you hold a large list but send to a limited number of people each day, Brevo is worth a close look.

Where MailerLite stands out

MailerLite is a natural shortlist option when your priority is a traditional newsletter workflow: collect subscribers, create campaigns, build signup forms, and run a few automated follow-ups. Its free tier includes up to 250 active subscribers and 2,500 emails per 30-day period. The free plan also includes up to three active automations, three forms, one landing page, and two account seats.

The key limitation is the active-subscriber cap. MailerLite counts active subscribers during the billing cycle, so deleting an address after it was active may not immediately make room under the same cycle's limit. If your list is likely to grow past 250 soon, check the paid subscriber tiers before you build your whole process around the free plan.

MailerLite's Comfort plan starts at $12 per month and expands the available tools and limits. The provider lists up to 50 automations and three user seats on Comfort, with exact sending allowance determined by subscriber tier. This is a more appropriate comparison for businesses that want to run multiple campaigns and journeys.

Where Brevo stands out

Brevo's free plan is unusual in the way it separates stored contacts from daily email sending. It allows up to 100,000 contacts in the account, but only 300 email sends per day. The free plan includes email campaigns, basic reporting, one marketing seat, and multi-channel marketing automation for up to 2,000 contacts.

This can make Brevo attractive if your contact database is large but you do not need to send a campaign to everyone at once. There is an important catch: a campaign with more than 300 recipients will only reach 300 people on the scheduled day on the free plan. You would need to requeue the remaining recipients on later days, or move to a paid tier.

Brevo's Starter plan starts at $9 per month. The first tier includes 5,000 monthly email sends and storage for up to 500 contacts; higher tiers change both the send allowance and contact capacity. Removing Brevo branding from Starter emails is a $9-per-month add-on. Standard starts at $18 per month and includes additional features such as a landing page.

Which one is better for your situation?

Choose MailerLite if…

  • Your newsletter list is under 250 active subscribers and you want to start without a paid subscription.
  • You value a focused newsletter, form, and landing-page workflow.
  • You want up to three free automations and can work within the monthly sending allowance.
  • You expect to pay for Comfort as your audience grows and need more active automations.

Choose Brevo if…

  • You need to store a large contact database but your daily campaign sends are modest.
  • You are comfortable planning around the 300-sends-per-day free limit.
  • You want to explore a broader customer communication platform with email, SMS add-ons, and other channels.
  • You may later need a paid plan based on monthly email volume and contact capacity.

What to check before choosing

  1. Count contacts the way the provider does. MailerLite's free cap is active subscribers; Brevo's free plan allows stored contacts up to its limit. These are different measures.
  2. Calculate a realistic send month. Estimate campaign frequency multiplied by recipients, plus welcome emails and automated follow-ups.
  3. Map the automation you actually need. List each journey, its trigger, and the number of steps. Check whether the plan allows enough active workflows and contacts to enter them.
  4. Include branding and seats. Check whether the free plan adds provider branding, whether additional users cost extra, and whether removing branding is an add-on.
  5. Check what happens when you grow. Compare the next paid tier, not only the free plan. Confirm how overages or automatic tier changes are handled.
  6. Use permission-based lists. Do not buy or scrape subscriber lists. Follow the provider's anti-spam rules and applicable email marketing laws.

Frequently asked questions

Is MailerLite or Brevo better for a free newsletter?

For a small list of fewer than 250 active subscribers and a monthly email pattern, MailerLite's allowance may be easier to work with. Brevo can store far more contacts for free, but its 300-email daily limit makes it less convenient for a single large campaign.

Can Brevo really store 100,000 contacts for free?

Brevo's current free-plan documentation lists storage for up to 100,000 contacts. That does not mean you can email all of them in one day: free email sending is limited to 300 sends per day, and only 2,000 unique contacts can enter active automations.

Does MailerLite limit free subscribers to 250?

Yes. MailerLite currently lists 250 active subscribers and 2,500 monthly email sends on its Free plan. It also limits the number of active automations, forms, and landing pages.

Which has cheaper paid plans?

Brevo's Starter plan starts at $9 per month and MailerLite's Comfort plan starts at $12 per month. Those starting prices do not buy the same allowances or features. Compare the actual contact count, monthly email volume, workflow needs, user seats, and any add-ons before calling one cheaper for your business.

Final verdict

MailerLite is a good first comparison for a small business that wants a straightforward newsletter and has a small audience. Brevo is more interesting when the contact database is large but daily email sends are modest. The free-plan limits make the decision fairly clear once you know how many people you need to email, how often you send, and how many automated journeys you want.
