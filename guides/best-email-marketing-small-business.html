Best Email Marketing Software for Small Businesses (2026)

A practical comparison of MailerLite, Brevo, GetResponse, and Mailchimp · US pricing in USD

The best email marketing software for a small business is the one that fits its list size, sending habits, and follow-up process without creating unnecessary cost or complexity. A solo consultant sending one newsletter a month has different needs from an online store sending order-related campaigns and automated promotions.

For a simple newsletter and a small starting list, MailerLite is a sensible place to begin. Brevo is worth a look when you want to store a large contact list but send fewer emails each day. GetResponse is a stronger fit to evaluate when you need more campaign and funnel tools. Mailchimp may suit businesses that value its established marketing ecosystem and integrations.

There is no universal winner. This guide explains the trade-offs so you can shortlist the right platform before paying.

1. MailerLite: a practical starting point for newsletters

Best for: small businesses, independent professionals, and creators who mainly need newsletters, signup forms, and a manageable level of automation.

MailerLite keeps its core email workflow relatively focused. Its current Free plan allows up to 250 active subscribers and 2,500 email sends per 30-day period. The free tier also limits how many automations, forms, and landing pages can be active. The paid Comfort plan starts at $12 per month at the 500-subscriber level on the provider's listed monthly pricing.

Why consider it: The free plan can be enough to learn the basics and send a modest newsletter. MailerLite also lists landing pages, signup forms, templates, and visual automation tools, so a small business can build a simple lead-capture and welcome-email workflow without assembling several separate services.

What to watch: The free plan's 250-active-subscriber ceiling is easy to outgrow if you already have a list. Check the current subscriber tier and the monthly sending allowance before importing contacts. Do not assume that a free-plan feature is unlimited just because it appears in the product interface.

2. Brevo: worth considering when contact storage matters

Best for: a business that has many contacts but does not need to email all of them every day.

Brevo's free plan is structured around sending volume rather than a small stored-contact cap: its help documentation lists up to 100,000 stored contacts and 300 email sends per day. That can be useful for a business that wants to keep a broad contact database but sends smaller campaigns in batches. Brevo also offers a wider customer communication suite, including email and other channels.

Why consider it: The daily allowance is easy to understand for a small operation with modest sending needs. Brevo's paid Standard plan starts at $18 per month and adds capabilities such as marketing automation, landing pages, and more reporting features, according to its plan documentation.

What to watch: A 300-email daily limit is not the same as 300 emails per month, and it is not unlimited campaign delivery. If you need to send a large newsletter to your whole audience at once, calculate how many days the free plan would require. Check the paid send-volume tier and any optional add-ons.

3. GetResponse: for campaigns with more moving parts

Best for: small businesses and creators who want email campaigns alongside landing pages and automated subscriber journeys.

GetResponse positions itself as a broader marketing platform rather than only a newsletter sender. Its Starter plan currently lists unlimited monthly email sends, AI-assisted content tools, signup forms and pop-ups, a landing page builder, and one custom automation workflow. The listed price is $19 per month with monthly billing or $15.58 per month when billed annually. The Marketer tier lists unlimited automation workflows and advanced audience segmentation at a higher price.

Why consider it: If your workflow is more than a periodic newsletter—for example, a signup page followed by a welcome series and several follow-up messages—it is worth comparing GetResponse's included automation tools with simpler services.

What to watch: One workflow may be enough for a simple funnel, but it may not cover several campaigns with different triggers. More advanced automation and segmentation sit on higher tiers. Compare the exact features included at your list size, not just the advertised starting price. Annual billing reduces the displayed monthly equivalent but commits you to a longer billing period.

4. Mailchimp: for businesses that value a broad ecosystem

Best for: businesses that want a widely recognized email marketing platform and may benefit from its templates, integrations, and connected marketing tools.

Mailchimp's pricing page currently lists a Free plan with up to 250 contacts and 500 email sends per month. Its Essentials plan starts at $13 per month, while Standard starts at $20 per month at the entry audience tier shown on its pricing page. The plan you actually pay for depends on the contact count and selected billing terms.

Why consider it: Mailchimp can make sense when the integrations, templates, and familiarity of its ecosystem are a priority. If your business already uses services that connect directly to Mailchimp, that compatibility may matter more than a small difference in the advertised entry price.

What to watch: Free-plan limits are modest, and the cost can rise as your contact count expands. Before choosing it, check which automation, segmentation, and reporting features are included in the specific tier you need—not just the entry-level offer.

Which email marketing platform should you choose?

  • Choose MailerLite if you want to get a basic newsletter and signup workflow running with minimal complexity, and your list is still small enough for its free plan.
  • Choose Brevo if you want to store a large number of contacts and your daily email volume is relatively low—or if its broader communication tools fit your business.
  • Choose GetResponse if landing pages and automated follow-up sequences are central to how you attract and nurture leads.
  • Choose Mailchimp if its integrations, templates, and existing place in your business workflow are especially valuable.

Before you decide, estimate how many active contacts you will have in six months, how many messages you will send in a typical month, and how many separate automated journeys you need. Those three numbers will eliminate many unsuitable plans.

How to compare the real cost

The advertised starting price is only one part of the decision. Build a simple estimate before entering a payment method:

  1. List size: Does the provider charge by active contacts, stored contacts, or another audience measure? What happens when you cross a tier?
  2. Sending volume: Is the limit per day, per month, or tied to your number of subscribers? Are transactional and marketing emails counted together?
  3. Automation: How many workflows, triggers, and actions are included? Will you need a higher tier as your process grows?
  4. Seats and extras: Does the plan charge more for additional users, premium integrations, SMS, or landing pages?
  5. Billing terms: Compare monthly and annual totals, renewal prices, taxes, and cancellation rules—not only a discounted monthly equivalent.
Simple buying rule: Do not upgrade for features you cannot name a use for. Start with the smallest plan that supports your current workflow, but make sure the next pricing tier is affordable if your list grows.

What every small business should check before sending

Choosing a provider does not guarantee that emails will reach the inbox. Before you launch a campaign, make sure the basics are in place:

  • Permission: Build your list through clear signups and other valid permission-based methods. Do not buy or scrape email lists.
  • Domain authentication: Follow the provider's instructions for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC where applicable. These records help receiving email services verify your sending domain.
  • Unsubscribe process: Make it easy for recipients to stop receiving marketing messages and honor opt-outs promptly.
  • List hygiene: Remove invalid addresses and monitor bounces, spam complaints, and engagement.
  • Test messages: Preview emails on mobile, check links, and send a test to more than one email provider before a large campaign.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free email marketing software for a small business?

It depends on your audience and sending pattern. MailerLite's free plan has 250 active subscribers and 2,500 monthly email sends. Brevo's free plan allows up to 300 sends per day and stores up to 100,000 contacts. Compare the limit that will constrain your actual workflow first.

Is email marketing software worth paying for?

It can be, when you have a clear reason to email customers or leads regularly and the platform saves enough time or supports enough revenue to justify the subscription. If you only need an occasional update to a tiny list, a free plan may be adequate.

How many email subscribers do I need to start?

You do not need a large list to begin. Start with people who have a clear reason to hear from your business and have agreed to receive your messages. A small, relevant list is more useful than a large list of people who did not ask to hear from you.

Can I switch email marketing platforms later?

Usually, you can export contacts, but migration details differ. Before committing, check whether you can export subscriber fields, tags, consent records, and suppression lists, and whether automations need to be rebuilt manually.

Bottom line

For a simple newsletter, start by comparing MailerLite's free plan with your list size. For a large stored contact list with modest daily sends, evaluate Brevo. For landing pages and multi-step campaign workflows, compare GetResponse's automation tiers. If integrations and a familiar ecosystem are important, include Mailchimp in your shortlist.

The best choice is not the platform with the most features. It is the one that meets your current needs, keeps the total cost predictable, and leaves room to grow without forcing you into tools you will not use.
