Best Free CRM for Freelancers in 2026

A practical comparison of free CRM plans · US pricing in USD

When you freelance, client details can end up scattered across email, spreadsheets, messages, and calendar reminders. A customer relationship management (CRM) tool gives you one place to track prospects, conversations, follow-ups, and potential projects.

The best free CRM is not necessarily the one with the longest feature list. It is the one that helps you remember who needs a reply, see which opportunities are active, and keep your client pipeline organized without adding more admin work.

For a freelancer who wants a broad set of sales and contact tools, HubSpot's free CRM is a strong starting point to evaluate. Bigin by Zoho is worth considering if you prefer a simple, single-user pipeline. Zoho CRM has a free edition for up to three users, while Capsule keeps contact and opportunity management relatively focused.

Quick recommendation: Start by listing the jobs you need your CRM to do: store contacts, track deals, schedule follow-ups, or manage a small team. Then compare the free-plan limits that matter to that workflow. A free plan can be useful, but it may not include proposals, contracts, invoicing, or every automation you expect.

1. HubSpot CRM: a broad free starting point

Could suit: freelancers who want to organize contacts and deals while keeping the option to add more sales or marketing tools later.

HubSpot's official free-tools page lists a free tier for up to two users and 1,000 contacts, with no credit card required. Its free CRM capabilities include contact management, deal pipelines, tasks and activities, CRM import, and reporting tools. The wider free toolset also lists features such as meeting scheduling, forms, and live chat, although individual tools have their own limits and branding.

Why consider it: A freelancer can use contact records to keep notes about a prospect, track an opportunity through a sales pipeline, and record the next follow-up task. This can be more reliable than trying to remember every conversation in an inbox.

What to watch: HubSpot's free tools are not the same as every feature in its paid Sales, Marketing, or Service products. The provider lists restrictions on users, contacts, records, email sends, and automation. Check the exact limit for the tool you intend to use, especially before building a more complicated process around it.

2. Bigin by Zoho: a simple pipeline for one-person businesses

Could suit: a solo freelancer who wants a compact way to track leads and client opportunities without starting with a large CRM suite.

Bigin's official pricing page lists a free plan for one user with one pipeline and up to 500 records. It also lists three automations in the free edition. This is a relatively clear starting point if your main goal is to move opportunities through stages such as New lead, Contacted, Proposal sent, Won, or Not proceeding.

Why consider it: The single-user plan is designed around an individual workflow. A simple pipeline can make it easier to see which prospects need attention and which projects have moved forward.

What to watch: The free plan's 500-record limit and single pipeline may be restrictive if you manage many contacts or separate service lines. Check what Bigin counts as a record and whether the integrations you need are included in the free tier.

3. Zoho CRM: a free option for a small freelance team

Could suit: two- or three-person freelance partnerships that need a more configurable sales CRM than a simple contact list.

Zoho's US pricing page lists a free edition for up to three users. The listed free features include contact management, follow-up reminders, workflow automation, custom email templates, tasks, meetings, calls, standard reports, and mobile apps.

Why consider it: The free user allowance can work for a small partnership where more than one person needs access to leads and follow-up activity. Zoho also offers a wider collection of business applications, which may be useful if your operation grows and you want to investigate connected tools.

What to watch: More configuration and options can mean more setup. If you are a solo freelancer with a small number of clients, a simpler CRM may be easier to maintain. Verify the current record limits and feature restrictions for the free edition in the official comparison.

4. Capsule CRM: focused contact and opportunity tracking

Could suit: freelancers who want a tidy contact database and one sales pipeline rather than a large all-in-one marketing platform.

Capsule's official pricing page lists a free plan for up to two users, with 250 contacts, five custom fields, and one sales pipeline. The free plan is intentionally limited compared with its paid tiers, but it may be enough for a freelancer with a small client list and a straightforward sales process.

Why consider it: A smaller, focused contact list can be easier to keep accurate. Capsule may be worth testing if your priority is tracking relationships and opportunities rather than managing a complex marketing operation.

What to watch: The 250-contact limit is the key constraint. Capsule's advanced automations and additional capabilities sit on paid plans. Check whether the free plan includes the integrations and reporting you need before moving your records into it.

How to choose the right free CRM as a freelancer

If you mainly need a place to organize leads

Look for fast contact entry, notes, reminders, and a clear view of your next action. Do not pay for complex automation if a simple follow-up task solves the problem.

If you manage several projects or client opportunities

Prioritize a visual pipeline and the ability to create stages that match your process. Check whether the free plan allows enough pipelines, records, and custom fields.

If you work with a partner or small team

Check the number of included users and how permissions work. A free plan that allows only one login may not suit a shared client workflow.

If you need proposals, contracts, or invoices

Do not assume a CRM includes a complete freelance business toolkit. These products primarily organize customer relationships and sales activity. You may need separate proposal, e-signature, accounting, or invoicing software, and you should check integrations before choosing.

A simple CRM setup for a freelancer

  1. Import only useful contacts. Start with active prospects, current clients, and recent customers rather than dumping every old address into the system.
  2. Create a small pipeline. For example: New lead → Contacted → Discovery → Proposal sent → Won / Lost.
  3. Record the next action. Every active opportunity should have a clear follow-up task or a reason it is waiting.
  4. Add context after conversations. Keep short, factual notes about the client's needs, timeline, budget, and agreed next step.
  5. Review it once a week. Update stale opportunities, close lost deals, and make sure no interested prospect has been forgotten.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free CRM for a solo freelancer?

There is no universal winner. HubSpot is a broad option to evaluate if you want contact and deal tools with a larger free contact allowance. Bigin may be a better fit if you prefer a single-user pipeline with a clear record limit. Choose based on the workflow you will actually maintain.

Is a free CRM really free?

The providers listed here advertise free plans, not just short trials. However, each free plan has feature or usage limits. You may need a paid tier as your contact list, team, or automation needs grow.

Does a freelancer need a CRM?

Not every freelancer does. If you have only a few clients and rarely manage new leads, a spreadsheet and calendar may be enough. A CRM becomes more useful when you regularly follow up with prospects, manage several opportunities, or need a shared record of client conversations.

Does CRM software replace invoicing software?

Usually not. Some business platforms connect to accounting or invoicing products, but you should verify the specific integration and plan requirements. Do not assume that contact and deal tracking includes tax, invoicing, or bookkeeping features.
